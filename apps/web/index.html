Bring your measurements and check a claim

Dr. Bean tests a claimed range for a drug's elimination rate against your concentration measurements. It searches for parameter values that fit every reading within its allowance but fall outside the claim, then helps you choose the next measurement that would tell two explanations apart.

Start a study

You will review the measurements and the claim, approve one bounded check, and read what the checker found.

Enter measurements myself

The example is synthetic: five readings between 0.25 and 4 hours, each allowed ±0.50 mg/L, testing the claim that the elimination rate lies between 0.10 and 0.17 per hour.

Model
Two-rate concentration curve, C(t) = A·(e−ke·t − e−ka·t)
Units
Elapsed hours, mg/L, rates per hour
Data you can bring
Typed values, a CSV with time,concentration,epsilon, or a PNG/JPEG lab sheet read under review
Who decides what
You review inputs and approve each operation. The Counterdex checker decides mathematical results. AI never does.

Four words you will see

Claim
The closed range you are testing, for example “ke is between 0.10 and 0.17 per hour.”
Allowance
The largest gap you accept between a curve and a reading. It is a hard bound, not a standard deviation.
Candidate
One set of parameter values (A, ke, ka) whose curve stays within every reading's allowance.
Counterexample
A candidate whose ke lies outside the claim. It shows your data does not force the claim. It does not reveal the true value.

    About this dataset

    The claim being tested

    Concentration starts at zero and absorption is faster than elimination (ka > ke). Both endpoints of the claim are included.

    Search limits

    The checker only considers parameter values inside these ranges. Wider limits search more; any result holds only within them.

    Model equation

    C(t) = A × (exp(−ke × t) − exp(−ka × t))
